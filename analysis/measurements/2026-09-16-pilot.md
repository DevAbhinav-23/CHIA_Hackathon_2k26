**Run date** 2026-09-16 (IST), on `cachyos-x8664`, on the five-container
`circt_bug_loop/cluster_single.yaml` cluster, submitted through
`circt_bug_loop/bug_loop_submit.sh`. **Scope** the first campaign-shaped live

## 0. Verdict

**The loop works, it found a real CIRCT assertion failure on its first seed, and
it spent USD 7.163957 against a registered cap of USD 5.00 - 43.3 % over.**

The overshoot is one defect and it is the only one that matters here: W1's
`SpendGuard` pre-authorises **one `generate_content` call**, and a turn that
carries tools makes up to `max_tool_iterations` of them, re-sending the whole
growing conversation each time. The three turns that were billed made **14, 21
and 37** calls and cost **19.7x, 32.3x and 64.1x** what the guard authorised.
`SpendGuard`'s own docstring says the authorised figure is "a FLOOR and not the
true worst case" for such a turn; this is the first measurement of how far below.

Seven more defects were found and fixed before the run could start at all, and
**not one of them is about the model**. Every one of them had been invisible
because nothing had ever driven `run_campaign`'s pre-flight on a cluster:
W-19a's smoke never ran it and W-19b's tier-3 tests drive `campaign_drive`
directly.

**The cause, exactly.** `SpendGuard.worst_case_usd` is
`(prompt_chars / 2.0 + 16000)` at the two prices, and that bounds **one**
`generate_content` call. A turn with tools runs CHIA's own loop of up to
`max_tool_iterations` = 100 calls, and every call re-sends the whole
conversation so far, so the input tokens grow roughly quadratically in the
number of tool calls: 14 calls billed 1.58 M input tokens against a 6 KB
prompt. `SpendGuard`'s docstring already says the authorisation is "a FLOOR and
not the true worst case" for such a turn and that the overshoot is "bounded by
one turn"; **measured, one turn is 64 times the bound**, and three turns took
the campaign 43 % past its cap.

**What did work.** The cap is not decorative: the next authorisation after the
spend was read refused, `turn_usage` returned NULL counts for the refused turns
rather than zeros (K10), `ledger.price` returned no price for them, the arm
stopped with `stop_reason="campaign_spend_cap"`, the mutation arm never started,
and `finish_run` wrote `ended_utc`. The loop stopped itself at 13:18:02 UTC -
**before** the operator's own `ray job stop`, which landed at 13:20:45 and found
the driver already past its last stage. The control works; its estimate is
wrong by one to two orders of magnitude.

## 11. What this pilot says about the campaign

1. **The USD cap must be lowered, or the guard must count tool calls.** At
   38.7x, a campaign registered at USD 200 can spend USD 7,700 before the
   pre-authorisation notices. The three candidate fixes, in increasing order of
   honesty: multiply the authorisation by `max_tool_iterations`; authorise
   **per `generate_content` call** inside `llm_turn` rather than per turn in
   `dispatch_turn`; or register a cap that the measured per-turn cost of this
   pilot - USD 1.2 to 4.0 a turn, not USD 0.06 - can be divided into. It is the
   architect's call and it is the one thing W-23 cannot be started without.
2. **A seed costs about USD 3.2 in its first iteration**, on this evidence: one
   seed's two A3 turns cost 3.207 and its first report turn 3.957. The
   registered campaign's 187 seeds at three iterations is not a USD 200 run.
3. **The seeded arm finds things.** First seed, first iteration, three probes,
   three firings, one distinct fingerprint, one screen against a live mirror
   that matched an open issue. That is the loop's whole thesis working once.
4. **The mutation arm is still unmeasured live.** It has a frozen set of 25
   mutators that fire in all three kinds offline (§10.6 of the synthesis
   record), and no campaign has yet reached it.

## P2.3 What the authorisation got wrong, and why it still held

`SpendGuard` bounds one tool result at `TOOL_OUTPUT_TOKENS_CAP`. That constant
was **32768**, from pilot 1's single measurement of about 16,900 tokens per
result. Pilot 2 measures the same quantity twice more, by solving
`6P + 15T = tokens_in` at `P = (prompt + system) / CHARS_PER_TOKEN`:

| seed | prompt + system | tokens in | **T, tokens per tool result** |
|---|---|---|---|
| `b3b407b37bb2…` | 6,271 | 459,484 | **29,378** |
| `42d11d06acf7…` | 10,722 | 576,454 | **36,286** |

**36,286 is 11 % above the constant**, so on seed 2 the input side of the
estimate was too low and 32768 was not a cap. The authorisation held anyway
because the OUTPUT side over-estimates enormously: 6 × 16,000 = 96,000 output
tokens authorised against 3,302 billed, worth USD 0.348 of the 0.753. That is
luck and not design, so the constant is **65536** now, the largest of the three
measurements rounded up to the next power of two. Errata row 46.
